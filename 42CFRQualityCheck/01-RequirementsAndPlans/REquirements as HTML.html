42 CFR Part 2 — Candidate Identification

Building a research list for our Qualified Entities: sources whose CCD data shows patterns consistent with substance use disorder treatment, so the QE can look into whether the data needs the confidentiality protections 42 CFR Part 2 requires.

The Question We're Answering

42 CFR Part 2 protects patient records from federally assisted substance use disorder programs with extra confidentiality requirements — separate consent, storage, and access controls. If a Part 2 program's data flows into the general CCD pipeline instead of the protected one, that's a compliance gap worth knowing about.

The catch: we can't tell from a CCD alone whether a source is legally a Part 2 program — that requires knowing things like federal funding status and how the facility holds itself out, which aren't visible in clinical data. What we can do is look for clinical content patterns that are consistent with SUD treatment activity, and use that to build a short list of sources worth a phone call.

This is a candidate-finding tool, not a compliance verdict. We surface the signal; the Qualified Entity does the research and makes the actual call.

Why This Matters

  • Compliance exposure: Part 2 data sitting in the general bucket is queryable without the extra protections federal law requires, regardless of whether anyone has actually queried it yet.
  • Patient trust: Part 2 exists because substance use patients face real stigma. A misrouted source undermines the consent framework those patients were told they could rely on.
  • We can't fix what we don't know about: the goal here is visibility — giving QEs a prioritized, evidence-backed list of sources to look into, rather than waiting for an audit to surface the issue.
  • A low bar for inclusion is the right call: a source that turns out to be fine after a QE looks into it costs us a conversation. A source we never flag because our bar was too high costs us a compliance gap we don't know exists. We'd rather ask a few extra questions than miss the real ones.

What We Look For

Each CCD is checked for a handful of substance-use treatment signals: diagnoses tied to opioid, alcohol, or other substance use disorders; medications used specifically to treat those conditions; treatment-type encounters (detox, intensive outpatient, opioid treatment program visits); and related procedures like drug screening. We also note the facility name and the specific care location within it, since a hospital can contain a dedicated SUD unit even when the hospital overall looks like ordinary general care.

Not every signal counts the same. Some medications (like methadone) are only used in a narrow, tightly regulated treatment context and are a strong signal on their own. Others (like buprenorphine) are also prescribed routinely in general practice, so they carry less weight by themselves. We also only count activity tied to the visit itself — a patient mentioning "I take methadone from my clinic" during an unrelated ER visit is not evidence that the ER is a Part 2 program.

Approach

Unit of Analysis
Each CCD gets its own score — plus a rollup by source, and by care location within a source
Priority Case
A source or unit that looks like ordinary care but shows a concentrated SUD treatment pattern
Bar for the List
Deliberately low — inclusion is cheap (a phone call), exclusion of a real case is not
Before Trusting the Results
We first test the approach against sources already known to be Part 2, to confirm it reliably picks them up

Workflow

1
Sample CCDs from each source — same sampling approach used across our other data quality work.
2
Score each CCD for the presence of SUD treatment signals tied to that specific visit — not the patient's broader history.
3
Roll results up two ways — by source overall, and by the specific care location within a source, so a treatment unit inside a general facility doesn't get diluted out of view.
4
Validate against sources we already know are Part 2 before trusting the approach on the broader population — if known Part 2 sources don't score as candidates, we refine the approach first.
5
Build the candidate list — sources and locations whose pattern is consistent with SUD treatment activity, prioritized by how generic the facility name looks (a source that already sounds like a treatment center is less of a discovery than one that doesn't).
6
Send each QE a summary explaining what we found for their source and why it's worth a look, along with the source's current S3 routing for context.

What Comes Out of This

Output Audience Purpose

A short write-up per candidate source The Qualified Entity Explains what we found and why it's worth researching, and asks the QE to look into it and report back
The QE write-up is a request for research, not an accusation — it says what we observed, why it's a pattern worth asking about, and makes clear that only the QE's follow-up can actually confirm Part 2 status.

Edge Cases We're Accounting For

  • A treatment unit inside a general facility: a hospital can have a dedicated detox or addiction medicine unit that behaves like a Part 2 program even though the hospital as a whole looks like ordinary acute care. We look at the specific care location, not just the source overall, so this doesn't get averaged away.
  • Behavioral health vs. substance use: a facility with a lot of general psychiatric content but little substance-use content isn't a Part 2 signal — we're specifically looking for substance use disorder patterns, not mental health broadly.
  • Routine office-based treatment: a primary care practice where a physician occasionally prescribes buprenorphine isn't automatically a Part 2 program. We weigh that signal lightly and look for stronger corroborating evidence before it affects a source's candidacy.
  • Facility name isn't the whole story: an obviously-named recovery center showing a strong pattern is a confirmation, not a discovery — we're most interested in sources with a generic or unrelated name showing the same pattern, since that's the case most likely to have been missed.
  • Patient history vs. this visit: a patient telling a provider about past or outside substance use treatment doesn't make that provider a Part 2 program. We only count activity tied to care actually delivered at that visit.

What Happens With the Candidate List

Result What Happens
Source or location shows a strong SUD pattern The QE receives a write-up and is asked to research the source and report back on whether it operates as, or contains, a Part 2 program.
QE confirms it is a Part 2 program The QE and PDR coordinate on rerouting the data to the protected pipeline going forward.
QE researches and determines it is not a Part 2 program Closed out — no further action. This is an expected and acceptable outcome, not a failure of the approach.
Source shows only a weak or borderline pattern Still included, but flagged as lower priority — worth a lighter check rather than an urgent one.
This tool does not move data or change access controls on its own. It builds a research list. The QE's research and any resulting routing change are separate, deliberate steps that happen after this process.
