Our Methodology

The PDR developed an automated scoring tool that examines the clinical content of CCD (Continuity of Care Document) files to identify patterns consistent with substance use disorder treatment activity. The methodology was developed and validated in three phases before being applied to this source:

Phase 1 — Calibration Against Known 42 CFR Sources

We first ran the tool against sources that QEs have already submitted to PDR's designated 42 CFR endpoint — sources that are known to be Part 2 programs. We sampled 20 CCDs from each of 14 known 42 CFR sources and confirmed the tool reliably scored them as candidates (average SUD prevalence among known Part 2 sources: 68%). This phase iterated through 3 refinement cycles until known Part 2 sources were consistently identified.

Phase 2 — General Population Sanity Check

We then ran the same tool against 200 sources from the general (non-42-CFR) pipeline to confirm it does not over-flag. Among general-population sources, the average SUD prevalence was 7.2%, with a maximum of 14.8%. This confirmed a clear separation between normal and treatment-focused sources.

Phase 3 — Candidate Identification

With confidence that the tool identifies known positives and does not flag obvious negatives, we ran the broader candidate scan across all sources submitting to the general pipeline. Of 4,745 sources evaluated, 23 were classified as candidates warranting QE research. Hudson Valley Wellness Associates was one of these 23.

What the Tool Looks For

For each CCD, we count indicators of SUD treatment activity tied to that specific visit (not patient history reported from elsewhere):

  • Diagnoses: ICD-10 codes F10–F19 (substance use disorders), excluding F17 (nicotine, which is not Part 2 covered)
  • Medications: MAT drugs — methadone (strong signal, OTP-only), buprenorphine/suboxone, naltrexone, and others, weighted by regulatory context
  • Billing/procedure codes: OTP-specific HCPCS codes (H0020, S0109, H0015, G2067–G2078) and SUD counseling/screening codes
  • Encounter types: Detox, intensive outpatient, residential treatment, opioid treatment program visits

We specifically exclude patient-reported history (e.g., "patient states they take methadone from another clinic") and chronic problem-list entries not tied to the current encounter. Only clinical activity delivered at this visit counts as evidence that the facility is providing SUD treatment.

How We Classify

After scoring individual CCDs, we compute the percentage of a source's CCDs that contain any SUD treatment indicators (the "SUD prevalence"). We also look at service-location-level concentrations within a source to catch treatment units inside larger facilities.
